About

I am a master's student in Computational Linguistics at the University of Tübingen, trained in computer science at Harbin Institute of Technology. My work sits where LLM agents meet computer vision: I build systems that run in the real world, and the measurement that shows whether they actually work.

I care about results that hold up outside the setting that produced them: an agent that still answers correctly when its tools fail, a detector that still works in the next match, a method that transfers to data it has never seen. My agent TheoClaw is in daily use, and every change to it passes an evaluation harness with repeated runs (pass@k vs. pass^k), fault injection, bias-tested LLM judges and bootstrap non-inferiority tests; a compiled knowledge base raised its grounded statements from 67% to 92%. In robot vision, I replaced a hand-tuned detector with small learned models and raised recall on hand-labelled data from 40% to 82% at 95% precision.

Research interests

  • Reliable LLM agents: evaluation, grounding, robustness to tool failures, and LLM-as-judge that can be trusted
  • Evaluation methodology: decisions from few, expensive trials with statistics fixed in advance
  • Agents that write and improve programs, applied to vision and physical AI
  • 3D computer vision: 3D Gaussian Splatting and efficient scene representations, camera geometry, pose estimation and tracking

I am open to research assistant (HiWi) and working-student positions in Tübingen and Stuttgart, and happy to talk about collaborations.

Selected Projects

TheoClaw — grounded domain QA agent with an evaluation harness2026 – present
theoclaw.app · code

A tool-using agent that answers questions of faith from the Bible and the sources of several Christian traditions, following the asker's own church tradition, in Chinese, English and German, used by church members and pastors in Hong Kong and Tübingen. Hybrid retrieval over a compiled wiki plus BM25; scripture is filled in by a lookup tool rather than quoted from model memory. The knowledge base raised grounded statements from 67% to 92%; the answering model was chosen with a bootstrap non-inferiority test (margins fixed in advance) over 270 blind-scored answers.

RoboMaster robot vision2021 – 2026
HIT Competitive Robotics Team · code
  • Perception stage of an autonomous robot in C++/OpenCV with an industrial camera: colour segmentation, contour geometry and light-bar pairing for real-time target acquisition.
  • Replaced hand-written pairing rules with a pair MLP and a plate-number CNN (PyTorch → ONNX → OpenCV DNN, under 1 ms per frame): precision 82.5% → 95.1%, recall 40% → 82% on 218 hand-labelled frames under time-block cross-validation.
  • Tracking and 3D: Kalman tracker with χ² gating, planar PnP pose at 0.72 px median reprojection error, and the robot's floor path in metres from a single static camera.
  • Monocular camera calibration (0.398 px RMS), with distances validated against the floor to 0.13% scale error.
